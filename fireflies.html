Closing the post-meeting execution gap: transforming passive broadcast emails into accountable, consent-driven action items.

1. Context

This project was developed as my individual capstone during the Airtribe AI-First PM Program, coursework (Jul – Oct 2026). Please note: this is a concept project with no live pilot.

Fireflies.ai is an established AI meeting assistant that joins video conferences, records conversations, generates transcripts, and highlights key soundbites. While Fireflies excels at natural language transcription and summarizing spoken dialogue during the meeting, post-meeting operational execution reveals a significant product gap.

2. Problem

Meeting participants make numerous verbal commitments (e.g., “I will send the revised pricing schedule by Thursday” or “I'll follow up with the legal team tomorrow”). Fireflies' AI accurately extracts these commitments from transcripts. However, its delivery mechanism breaks down immediately after the call:

  • Unassigned Broadcast Emails: Fireflies sends a generic email summary to all meeting attendees containing an unstructured list of action items.
  • No Owner, Due Date, or Status: Commitments are presented without an assigned owner, clear due dates, or status tracking mechanisms.
  • The Bystander Effect: Because action items are broadcast collectively to the group, no single person takes explicit accountability. Everyone assumes someone else is handling it.
  • Baseline Failure: Internal workflow analysis revealed that only 28% of extracted commitments reach a resolved state within 14 days. The remaining 72% evaporate into email archives.

3. Users and Evidence

To identify the root cause of this follow-up decay, I conducted rigorous user discovery:

  • 19 In-Depth User Interviews across 3 Companies: Interviewed sales representatives managing prospect cycles, engineering team leads coordinating cross-functional releases, and startup founders driving client relationships.
  • Live Client Call Observation: Tested Fireflies on an actual commercial client call to evaluate real-time transcript extraction and track attendee behavior across the subsequent 14 days.
  • Key Finding — Visibility Breakage: Lack of visibility was the #1 operational breakdown cited by 8 out of 19 interviewees. Participants noted that once the post-call email leaves the top of their inbox, commitments are lost unless someone manually transcribes them into another system.
  • Consent Friction: Users strongly rejected automated, algorithmic task assignment. When AI blindly assigns a task to an individual without their consent, users feel micromanaged and ignore the notification.

4. Key Decisions and Trade-offs

I brainstormed and evaluated 14 distinct feature concepts, scoring each across Reach, Impact, Confidence, and Effort (RICE prioritization). Deciding what not to build was just as critical as defining the core feature set.

Documented “What Not to Build”

  • Did NOT build a standalone project management tool: Attempting to turn Fireflies into a full-fledged task tracker would create massive scope bloat and direct friction with existing primary workspaces. Fireflies must remain a focused, high-leverage meeting intelligence layer.
  • Did NOT build automated non-consensual assignment: User discovery proved that forcing tasks onto teammates without explicit consent causes resentment. We chose an opt-in claim/consent model where users confirm ownership with one click.

The Core Product Solution — Commitment Cards:

  • Per-Commitment Cards: Each AI-extracted commitment is encapsulated into an individual card displaying the speaker, quoted excerpt from the transcript, and an estimated timeframe.
  • Explicit Owner Consent: Suggested assignees receive an actionable prompt to claim or reassign the card. Accountability is accepted, never imposed.
  • “Waiting On” Dependency Tagging: Users can flag dependencies (e.g., “Waiting on client NDA signature”), making blocker visibility transparent across the team.
  • One-Click “Blocked” Button: An immediate escalation trigger that alerts the meeting organizer when an external dependency stalls progress.
  • Daily Digest View: A consolidated, distraction-free morning briefing summarizing only the commitments where the user is an owner, a dependency, or a watcher.

5. What I Built

To communicate the product requirements and validate user flows, I created three core deliverables:

  • Complete Product Requirement Document (PRD): Detailed user personas, functional specifications, state transition diagrams (Unclaimed → Claimed → In Progress → Blocked → Resolved), edge cases, and technical integration requirements.
  • 17-Slide Executive Pitch Deck: Structured market context, customer discovery insights, competitive positioning, unit economics, and roll-out milestones for executive review.
  • 6-Screen Interactive Clickable Prototype: Built a high-fidelity interactive prototype on Lovable modeling the end-to-end user experience across desktop and mobile viewports.

6. Outcome & Proposed Impact

As noted, this was an individual capstone project for the Airtribe AI-First PM Program (coursework, concept only with no live pilot). The proposed impact and success framework were evaluated through rigorous peer and mentor review:

  • North Star Metric: 14-day commitment resolution rate.
  • Baseline: 28% of extracted commitments currently reach a resolved state within 14 days.
  • Proposed Target: Lift the 14-day resolution rate from 28% to 45% within 6 months of general feature availability.
  • Secondary Metrics:
    • Owner claim rate: >70% of cards claimed or reassigned within 24 hours of call completion.
    • Blocked flag response time: <12 hours average turnaround time by meeting organizer upon receiving a blocked alert.
  • Artifact Evaluation: The PRD, slide deck, and 6-screen Lovable prototype demonstrated how AI transcription tools can transition from passive meeting recorders to active workflow facilitators.

7. What I Would Do Differently

Key reflections from conducting this product evaluation:

  • Interviewing Asynchronous Remote Teams: My 19 user interviews were concentrated among hybrid sales and management teams that rely heavily on synchronous video meetings. Interviewing fully asynchronous engineering teams would have surfaced additional requirements around automated time-zone handling and non-email notification digests.
  • Testing Micro-Interaction Notification Fatigue: While daily digests reduce clutter, the prototype did not test notification thresholds across high-volume users who participate in 6+ calls daily. Running a simulated notification frequency test would help establish optimal batching rules.
